🌌 The Fundamental Physical Problem

Can a truly two-dimensional monolayer crystal maintain spontaneous magnetic order at non-zero temperatures?

According to the celebrated Mermin-Wagner Theorem (1966), continuous rotational symmetry in 2D isotropic Heisenberg models guarantees that gapless Goldstone spin waves (magnons, ω ∝ k²) suffer a divergent thermal population:

n_magnon ∼ ∫ (k dk) / (e^{D k²/T} - 1) ∼ T ∫ (dk / k) → ∞ ⇒ T_c ≡ 0 K

Thermal fluctuations wash out long-range order for any T > 0. Yet in 2017, physicists discovered that monolayer CrI&sub3; is a robust ferromagnet up to T_c ≈ 45 K! How is this paradox resolved? Relativistic spin-orbit coupling (SOC) with heavy Iodine ligands induces an out-of-plane Magnetic Anisotropy Energy (MAE) barrier, opening a spectral gap Δ in the spin waves that cuts off the infrared divergence and rescues finite-temperature ferromagnetism.

🤖 Mapping DFT Energies to Effective Spin Models via Linear Regression

In Section 4 of Loh's notes, determining the exchange parameters ($J_1, J_2$) is formulated as a linear neural network or multivariate linear regression:

E({S}) = X₀ W₀ + X₁ W₁ + X₂ W₂ + ... = N E₀ - J₁ ∑⟨ij⟩₁ Sᵢ Sⱼ - J₂ ∑⟨ij⟩₂ Sᵢ Sⱼ

Here, for each spin configuration $n$, the features $X_b$ represent the number of parallel minus antiparallel bonds, and the weights $W_b$ are the unknown physical exchange constants. We solve $\mathbf{W} = (\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X})^{-1} \mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{E}_{\text{DFT}}$ to minimize the mean squared error $\chi^2$.

🧭 Magnetic Anisotropy Energy (MAE) & Spin-Orbit Coupling

In 2D isotropic systems, the Mermin-Wagner Theorem dictates that continuous $SO(3)$ spin rotations produce gapless Goldstone spin-waves ($\omega \propto k^2$) whose thermal fluctuations diverge, destroying long-range ferromagnetic order at any $T > 0$.

However, relativistic spin-orbit coupling (SOC) with heavy Iodine ligands ($Z=53$) couples the electron spins to the crystal lattice, breaking continuous $SO(3)$ symmetry down to a discrete out-of-plane $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry.

Energy E_MAE(θ) = E₀ + K sin²θ Easy Axis: θ = 0°, 180°
Interactive Polar Angle (θ) 0° (Out-of-Plane Up)
Anisotropy Energy Barrier: K = E(90°) - E(0°) ≈ 2.70 meV ≈ 31.4 K
Spin-Wave Activation Gap: Δ = 2 K S ≈ 94.2 K (for S = 3/2)
Why Anisotropy Restores 2D Magnetism:
The spin-wave dispersion becomes gapped: $$\omega(\mathbf{k}) = \Delta + D k^2$$ Because $\Delta > 0$, the thermal magnon integral $\int \frac{k dk}{e^{\omega/T} - 1}$ is regularized as $k \to 0$. The infrared divergence vanishes, enabling monolayer $\text{CrI}_3$ to sustain long-range ferromagnetism up to $T_c \approx 45\text{–}50\text{ K}$!

📈 Binder Cumulant Crossing: Pinpointing True Thermodynamic Tc

In any finite-size Monte Carlo simulation ($L < \infty$), the system spontaneously flips between $+M$ and $-M$ over long timescales, causing $\langle M \rangle = 0$. Furthermore, taking $\sqrt{\langle M^2 \rangle}$ yields a rounded curve that shifts with $L$.

To determine the true thermodynamic Curie temperature without finite-size distortion, Loh utilizes the 4th-order Binder Parameter:

B(T, L) = ⟨M⁴⟩ / ⟨M²⟩²
Binder Parameter Curves for L = 4, 8, 16 Crossing Point = Exact T_c
• At T ≪ Tc (Rigid Order): ⟨M⁴⟩ ≈ ⟨M²⟩² ⇒ B → 1.0
• At T ≫ Tc (Gaussian Noise): ⟨M⁴⟩ ≈ 3 ⟨M²⟩² ⇒ B → 3.0
• At T = Tc (Critical Point): Dimensionless scaling ensures B is independent of L!
Crossing Result in Attempt 1:
The curves for $L=4$, $L=8$, and $L=16$ all intersect at Tc ≈ 21.3 K.
This matches the exact analytical Onsager honeycomb solution: $$T_c = \frac{J_1}{\text{arccoth}(3)} = \frac{14.19\text{ K}}{0.65848} \approx 21.55\text{ K}$$ demonstrating the high precision of the finite-size scaling technique.

Attempt 4 Anisotropic Heisenberg Discrete ℤ₂ (Easy-axis) J₁, J₂, K = 31.4 K — ≈ 50 K 45 K Spin-orbit coupling creates MAE, opening a spin-wave gap Δ ~ K that rescues finite-T ferromagnetism!
Attempt 5 Quantum Heisenberg ℤ₂ + Quantum S=3/2 J₁, J₂, K, S=3/2 — ≈ 45 K 45 K Zero-point quantum spin fluctuations slightly suppress order from 50 K to the exact experimental 45 K.

4 Practical Pitfalls Highlighted by Loh:

  • ISIF=3 Vacuum Collapse: In 2D monolayer slabs, full stress relaxation with ISIF=3 collapses the vacuum gap along the c-axis. Always use ISIF=4 or ISIF=2.
  • DFT SCF Spin Flips: Asymmetric spin states (e.g. 7 up, 1 down) often flip back to FM during electronic relaxation. Always verify the output spin profile by projecting CHGCAR voxel magnetization, and damp magnetic mixing with AMIX_MAG and BMIX_MAG.
  • Design Matrix Conditioning: Ensure feature matrix rank equals number of unknowns, and pick spin configurations with near-orthogonal feature rows to prevent numerical instability.
  • Mean-Field Blind Faith: MFT neglects long-wavelength fluctuations, overestimating 2D critical temperatures by 50% to 100%. Always verify with Monte Carlo simulations!
